1. Explainability

The experimental set-up for explaining the crop type of each field: what an agronomist asks of an explanation, the ground truth the explanations are judged against, and the methods that produce them. Designed, not yet run. The field borders are not explained here.

  1. 1

    The agronomic questions

    An explanation of a field's crop is useful when an agronomist can check it. Three questions do that.

    When?Crop phenology

    Does the evidence fall at the right moment of the crop's phenology?

    • Every crop follows its own calendar of emergence, green-up, peak vegetation growth, flowering, ripening and harvest.
    • Crops differ most around peak vegetation growth and flowering, so that is where an explanation should fall.
    • In our 2021 profiles of Estonia, winter wheat and spring barley reach peak growth in June, peas in early July and potatoes in late August.
    Which property?What tells crops apart

    Does the evidence rest on a property of the plant or the soil that tells this crop apart? Each group of bands measures one.

    PropertyBandsHow it tells crops apart
    Greenness and canopy coverred, near infraredA growing crop against bare soil: winter crops are green in April, spring crops are not.
    Chlorophyllred-edgeCanopies that look equally green but differ in leaf chlorophyll and density.
    Canopy water and dry mattershortwave infraredA green, water-rich canopy against a ripening one: cereals ripen in July while grassland and potatoes stay green.
    Flower colourvisibleRapeseed in yellow flower against cereals.
    Canopy structureradar VHTall, branching, broad-leaved crops against cereals: rapeseed's return in late June is far stronger than the cereals'.
    Soil roughness and moistureradar VVBare, tilled or ridged soil before a late crop emerges, as with potatoes.
    Against which crop?The crops it is confused with

    Is it the cue that separates this crop from the ones it is confused with?

    • Winter wheat rather than spring wheat rests on a canopy in April.
    • Spring barley rather than spring wheat rests on a peak two to three weeks earlier.
  3. 2

    The agronomic ground truth

    What established agronomic knowledge says a model should rely on, written down per crop type and zone before any explanation is read.

    2.1External datasets to integrate

    Agro-ecological zones

    The zone of every field, so that one crop can have a different calendar in each zone.

    GAEZ v5 agro-ecological zones, already used in the transferability plan.

    The zoning is still to be determined

    Crop calendar maps

    Sowing, growing and harvest windows per crop and region.

    JRC ASAP crop calendar, WorldCereal crop calendars, national sources.

    Coverage of Estonia and of the 20 crop types not yet checked

    Theoretical band values

    The spectral and radar behaviour expected of a crop at each growth stage.

    Published Sentinel-2 and Sentinel-1 signatures per stage.

    Sources to be chosen

    • A sample: the reference will hold every crop type, per zone, with the source of each entry.

    2.3The assessment

    The last step of the ground truth. For each crop type, zone and method, the explanation is compared with the reference.

    • Months and bands: how much of the explanation falls in the expected months and bands, against what an even spread would give.
    • Direction: whether the crop's score moves the way the theoretical band values say it should.
    • Agreement with the ground truth says an explanation is agronomically plausible. Agreement between methods says it reflects the model, and not one method's behaviour.
  5. 3

    The experimentation

    Feature attribution methods, applied to the trained models, say how much each month and band contributed to a field's crop. Their explanations go back to the assessment (2.3), round after round: what one round shows sets the next.

    MethodFamilyWhat it givesCompared with
    OcclusionPerturbationHow much the field's crop score falls when a month or a band group is removed.Expected months and bands
    ALE, accumulated local effectsPerturbationHow a crop's score changes as the level of a band group in a month rises.Expected direction, from the theoretical band values
    SHAPDistillationEach month's and band group's share of the score, averaged over combinations of removed groups.Expected months and bands
    LIMEDistillationThe weights of a small linear model fitted around one field.Expected months and bands
    Expected or Integrated GradientsBackpropagationThe contribution of every band and month, from the gradients of the crop score.Expected months and bands

    What every method shares

    Unit
    The field. The explained quantity is the crop score averaged over the field.
    Against which crop
    The score of crop A minus the score of crop B, read from the same runs.
    Input groups
    Months, sensors and band groups.
    A removed month
    Replaced in two ways, which show different things: interpolated from its neighbours (events, such as a peak) and by the mean of that month over the training set (states, such as green in April).
    Models
    The five with image inputs: TerraMind, THOR, Prithvi-EO-2.0, TESSERA v2 and the baseline trained on the raw imagery. AlphaEarth is open: only its embedding is available, so these methods cannot act on its inputs.
